# Czech language comprehension – all training topics (detailed, EN)

**Purpose.** Complete prioritised list of 233 topics (T001–T233) for training oral Czech language comprehension (and the matching production) of children aged 3–7, for the TreninkPorozumeni app. Descriptions are in English, all examples are in Czech. Out of scope: writing and spelling topics, and articulation / speech-sound production training (e.g. correcting the pronunciation of R, Ř, L or sibilants). Phonemic topics train hearing and discrimination only; phonological-awareness and word-formation topics are kept because they are trained orally. The same list, order and T-IDs are used in topics_short_cz.md, topics_short_en.md and topics_detailed_cz.md.

Each topic lists: coverage by existing Documentation, age band + level label, score with its components, a short summary, a detailed description (what is trained, typical child errors, how to train orally, distractor/contrast), at least 3 Czech examples (phonemic topics: many pairs), sources.

## Ordering formula and scoring
- **Score = (frequency + importance) / difficulty**, rounded half-up to 2 decimals.
- **frequency (1–5):** 5 = in almost every child conversation; 3 = weekly; 1 = rare / metalinguistic only.
- **importance (1–5):** 5 = misunderstanding breaks sentence meaning or is a school-entry criterion; 3 = useful, meaning usually recoverable from context; 1–2 = stylistic.
- **difficulty (1–5, half-steps allowed):** rated per topic, independently of the age band, by how hard the contrast is for a child to perceive or understand. Anchors: 1.5 = salient concrete contrast mastered early; 2–2.5 = salient contrast that still needs attention; 3 = short unstressed grammatical cue or moderately close sounds; 3.5 = close sounds (voicing, sibilants; Bednářová & Šmardová 4.5–5 y) or relational meaning; 4 = very close sounds or abstract / deictic relations; 4.5 = metalinguistic tasks or rare complex syntax; 5 = phoneme manipulation, irony, jokes. Phonemic topics use the Czech perception norms as anchors where they exist; all other values are expert estimates. The detailed files give the reason for each topic's difficulty.
- **Age band** is a separate field (when the topic is typically trained / mastered); it does not enter the score.
- **🌐 L2-specific** = topic relevant only to children with another first language (Vietnamese, Ukrainian, Slovak). Ranked in the main list like any other topic; frequency and importance are rated for the whole target group.
- **Sort:** displayed score desc → importance desc → earlier start of age band → fixed internal catalogue order.

## Age bands and level labels
- Age bands 3–4 / 4–5 / 5–6 / 6–7 years; "4–5 → 5–6" = training starts in the first band and continues into the second. The basis of the band (sourced norm or estimate) is given in brackets in the detailed files.
- Level: **pre-school** (band starts 3–4, 4–5 or 5–6) / **first-grade entry** (starts 6–7) / **pre-school → first-grade entry** (starts pre-school, ends 6–7).

## Realistic note: estimates vs sourced norms
- **Sourced Czech norms (read in full):** phonemic discrimination with/without picture by Bednářová & Šmardová (sound 4/5 y, vowel 4/4.5–5, voicing+sibilants 4.5–5/5, length 5/5–5.5, palatal 5.5/5.5–6, syllables 6–6.5), auditory memory and phonological-awareness steps (syllable clapping 4, rhymes 5, initial sound 5, phoneme blending/segmentation 7), Škodová phonemic test (valid 4–7, feature norms), HSET-CZ sentence-comprehension split (<5 vs 5+: OVS, relatives, "dřív, než"), Lechta (why/when questions ~3.5; dysgrammatism of forms/gender/conjugation should end after 4; plural after 3).
- **Low-authority but read:** popular milestone pages (FN:S10, S11: on/ona/ono ~3; L, R, Ř stabilise by 7).
- **Estimates:** all other age bands — in particular almost all grammar, vocabulary, pragmatics, discourse and L2 topics — are agent estimates from general sources and the TypeScope hypotheses. Item-level Czech norms (TROG-2 CZ, TEPO block ages, Smolík & Seidlová-Málková tables) were not accessible.
- GB:* and C:* sources were read at search-snippet level only; F:GK, GB:GK, C:GK = general knowledge of the research agent without URL.
- Each record states its age basis in brackets after the band.
- Frequency, importance and difficulty are expert estimates, not measured data.

## Phonemic example markers
- **[REAL, <source>]** = the pair is printed in a Czech test/source read in full (Škodová et al. 1995 via FN:S1; Bednářová & Šmardová via FN:S2; Lechta via FN:S6; svet-logopedie FN:S9).
- **[SNIPPET]** = pair seen only in a search snippet (FN:N5).
- **[agent-compiled real-word pair]** = both words are real Czech words and differ in exactly one sound, but no test source lists the pair.
- A note after a semicolon refines any marker: **; name / names** = a name is used, **; rare word / rare form** = one word or form is rare, **; m/n**, **; medial** = which contrast or position is tested. A note in round brackets before the marker (e.g. „(p/b in two positions)“, „(differ in two lengths)“) flags a pair that differs in more than one place.
- **[no true minimal pair, near-pair/syllable]** = near-pair that differs in more than the tested sound; **[syllables]** = syllable material; **[syllable/pseudoword]** with **(pseudo)** = one member is a pseudoword; a pseudoword pair printed in a source keeps its source marker (e.g. míč × míš (pseudo) [REAL, svet-logopedie]). True real-word minimal pairs exist for every listed contrast except ď/j (dropped); for ř/ž (pařit–pažit), k/g and f/v only rare words or names are available, so syllables are used as well.

### T001 | Basic spatial prepositions in, on, under
*Czech name: Základní předložky místa v, na, pod*

- **Coverage:** ✅ COVERED — doc1 §5.2; doc3 §5.2 (item 2 „Míč leží pod židlí“); TS5.2 F1/F2
- **Age band:** 3–4 (popular milestone: in/on understood at 2, FN:S10; TS5.2 hypothesis v/na/pod 2–3 y)
- **Level:** pre-school
- **Score:** **6.67** = (frequency 5 + importance 5) / difficulty 1.5
- **Difficulty basis:** Easy: the relation is visible in the picture and v/na/pod are among the first prepositions children use.
- **Summary:** Child finds/places an object by v/na/pod, not by where the object usually is.
- **Detailed description:**
  - **Trains:** understanding of the earliest locative prepositions (v, na, pod + locative/instrumental) answering "kde?".
  - **Typical errors:** placing by prototype (an apple goes into the basket regardless of the word), not hearing the short unstressed preposition.
  - **Oral training:** placement instructions with toys, hide-and-seek ("Kde je medvídek?"), picture choice.
  - **Distractor/contrast:** the same objects in a different spatial relation (on vs under vs in).
- **Examples (Czech):**
  - „Míč leží pod židlí.“
  - „Na stole leží kniha.“
  - „Kočka je v krabici.“
  - „Dej medvídka pod stůl.“
- **Sources:** doc1 §5.2, doc3 §5.2.1, TS5.2; F:S1; CUR:F1, CUR:S14; LG:S10; FN:S10

### T002 | Imperative – comprehension and production
*Czech name: Rozkazovací způsob – porozumění a tvorba*

- **Coverage:** 🟡 PARTIAL — TS5.6 forms inventory: imperative listed as out of scope (not a tense / not depictable)
- **Age band:** 3–4 → 4–5 (estimate)
- **Level:** pre-school
- **Score:** **6.67** = (frequency 5 + importance 5) / difficulty 1.5
- **Difficulty basis:** Easy: a command is backed by gesture and situation, and imperatives are the first verb forms children react to.
- **Summary:** Child understands imperative instructions and forms pojď, dej, sedni si, nedělej.
- **Detailed description:**
  - **Trains:** imperative forms for ty / vy / my (pojď, pojďte, pojďme), irregular forms (jdi, vezmi) and the negative imperative, which is mostly imperfective ("nekřič").
  - **Typical errors:** an infinitive instead of the imperative ("dát!"), mixing singular and plural address, perfective negative ("nevezmi").
  - **Oral training:** "Simon says", the child gives instructions to a puppet or to a group.
  - **Distractor/contrast:** addressing one person vs a group, positive vs negative command.
- **Examples (Czech):**
  - „Pojď sem!“
  - „Sedněte si, děti.“
  - „Neber si to!“
