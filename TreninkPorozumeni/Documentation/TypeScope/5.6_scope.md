# 5.6 Scope (web scope explorer output)

## Type
5.6 „Slovesné časy" — comprehension of tense: a spoken Czech sentence, the child picks the picture whose visible state matches the tense (e.g. „Tatínek bude sekat trávu." vs „Tatínek posekal trávu."). Picture constraint (P8 conventions): tense is shown only by the state of the work — F (not started), R (in progress, half done), P (finished). Allowed pairs F–P and R–P; R–F forbidden; 1 gram + 2 lex distractors.

## Forms inventory
- Perfective past (l-participle, dokonavé): „Tatínek posekal trávu." — depictable: yes (P state: result complete)
- Imperfective past (nedokonavé): „Tatínek sekal trávu." — depictable: no/risky (means "was mowing" — fits an in-progress picture as well as finished; excluded)
- Imperfective present: „Tatínek seká trávu." — depictable: yes (R state: mid-action, half done)
- Analytic future „bude + imperfective infinitive": „Tatínek bude sekat trávu." — depictable: yes (F state: untouched material)
- Perfective future in present form (dokonavé sloveso, tvar přítomný, význam budoucí): „Tatínek poseká trávu." — depictable: yes (F state); linguistically harder because the form looks like present but means future
- Prefixed future of motion verbs (půjde, pojede, poletí): „Kluk pojede na kole." — depictable: risky (motion has no clear "result state"; avoid or use only with a clear goal object)
- Perfective past vs future of the same verb with aspect pair (sní / snědl) — the core aspect-tense interplay, depictable: yes
- Conditional / imperative / pluperfect-like forms: out of scope (not tenses / not depictable)

## Majority (marked) form
F–P contrast with the "textbook" forms: perfective past („posekal") and analytic future „bude + imperfective infinitive" („bude sekat"). These carry the most explicit tense cue (auxiliary „bude" vs l-participle). Acquisition: children use present first, past from about 3 years, past and future reliably around 4–5 years (sources below); comprehension of tense contrast is a 4–6-year diagnostic point.

## Minority form(s)
Recommended minority (two kinds, both counted per played sentence):
1. **Imperfective present as a variant sentence** in R–P items („Kuchař vaří polévku." vs „Kuchař uvařil polévku."). Reason: present is the earliest acquired, most frequent tense; without it children learn "present-looking sentence is never right / tense is only bude vs -l".
2. **Perfective future in present form** („Kluk postaví hrad.") in F–P items. Reason: the form is morphologically present but means future — tests the real form–meaning mapping instead of the shortcut "bude = future".

Concrete target (rule E = 32–34%):
- ~38 R–P mixed items, exactly one variant in present (other = perfective past) → 19 %.
- ~28 F–P mixed items where the future variant is perfective future (other = perfective past) → 14 %.
- Remaining ~34 F–P items: bude-future + perfective past (pure majority).
- E = (38 + 28) / 2 / 100 = **33 %**. Every group of 10 needs ≥ 6 minority-bearing items (≥ 3 expected); at most 4 pure-majority items in a row after the seeded shuffle.
- Note: this keeps the P8 split (~40 % R–P / ~60 % F–P) and implies ~45 % of F–P items use perfective future (vs. P8's ~50 %); the coordinator may shift a few items to hit 32–34 % exactly. If the coordinator counts only present as minority, it needs ~66 R–P items, which breaks the P8 split — not recommended.

## Contrast kept by minority items
Minority items still contrast two tenses on the same subject + object: present (half done) vs perfective past (finished), or perfective future (untouched) vs perfective past (finished). The gram distractor is always the other state of the pair; the swap rule holds (v2 in the other tense makes the gram picture correct). Lex pictures (other subject / other object) stay wrong for both sentences because tense changes neither. Past stays perfective only, so no imperfective-past ambiguity.
