# 5.8 Scope (web scope explorer output)

## Type
5.8 „Shoda přísudku s podmětem (Rodová shoda)" — doc1 §5.8 (subjectless past tense, „Skákala na trampolíně." / „Šel do obchodu."), doc3 §5.8 („Spadl ze stolu.", „Byla rozbitá.", „Utekli do lesa."). Picture constraint (coordinator): existing pictures = one actor doing an action, target vs gram picture are a mirrored pair differing only in actor gender (boy/man vs girl/woman). Minority must be realised by TEXT changes of variant v2 only.

## Forms inventory
- l-participle masc. sg. (verbal predicate, past): „Plaval v bazénu." — depictable: yes (current majority side for male targets)
- l-participle fem. sg.: „Plavala v bazénu." — depictable: yes
- l-participle neuter sg. („Plavalo v bazénu." — kotě/dítě): depictable: no with existing pictures (needs a neuter actor; boy/girl pair cannot show it)
- plural -li / -ly / -la („Plavali/Plavaly v bazénu."): depictable: no with existing pictures (one actor); also -li vs -ly is purely orthographic in speech (homophones) → not testable by audio at all
- compound past with auxiliary in 1st/2nd person („Plavala jsem."): depictable: risky (person shift, the child must map „já" onto the pictured actor)
- conditional („Plavala by v bazénu."): depictable: risky (hypothetical meaning conflicts with a picture of a real action); gender still on the l-participle, so no new contrast
- passive participle in predicate („Byla umytá." / „Je oblečený."): depictable: risky/no with existing action pictures (needs a resultant state of the actor; the pictured actor is the agent, not patient)
- jmenný přísudek with adjective, present („Je mokrá." / „Je unavený."): depictable: risky — gender marked by the adjective, but the adjective must be visibly true of the existing action picture (e.g. bathing → „mokrá"); only a minority of action pictures support such a state; also tends to lose the action, making lex distractors weaker
- doplněk agreeing with the subject, present tense („Plave v bazénu sama." / „Obouvá si boty sám."): depictable: yes (one actor alone is already what every picture shows; gender carried only by sám/sama)
- doplněk adjectival other than sám („Leží unavený.", „Přišla mokrá."): depictable: risky (state must be visible)
- explicit pronoun subject („Ona plave."): not agreement (lexical gender cue) — out of scope

## Majority (marked) form
Subjectless past tense with the l-participle in masc./fem. singular: „Plaval v bazénu." / „Plavala v bazénu." Typical acquisition: Czech children produce past-tense l-forms from about 2;0–2;6 and gender agreement on the verb is largely in place by 3–4 years; comprehension of subjectless sentences relying only on the ending is a typical 4–6-year diagnostic point (doc3 §5.8.1; persistent single default form is a dysphasia marker).

## Minority form(s)
Recommended: present tense + subject-oriented doplněk „sám / sama" carrying the only gender cue:
- Pattern: „[present verb] [same complement as v1] sám/sama." e.g. v1 „Plavala v bazénu." → v2 „Plave v bazénu sama."; „Obouval si boty." → „Obouvá si boty sám."
- Reason: works with every existing picture (the actor is alone in all of them), changes only v2 text, keeps the gender contrast audible on one inflected word (-Ø vs -a), and removes the l-participle so the child cannot rely on the habitual cue.
- Linguistic caveat (must be logged): „sám/sama" is a doplněk (subject complement with double dependency on subject and predicate), not the přísudek itself. Strictly, it is subject–COMPLEMENT agreement, i.e. same agreement mechanism (gender+number concord with the unexpressed subject) but a neighbouring sentence member. It is within the spirit of 5.8 (rodová shoda s podmětem), at the edge of the letter („shoda přísudku").
- Naturalness: natural and frequent in child-directed speech („Už se obléká sama!"). Risk: „sám" can also read as „without help / on his own" — fine, since one actor is shown in both pictures; avoid verbs where „sám" implies a missing helper that the picture should show.
- Alternative (strictly in scope, jmenný přísudek): „Je mokrá." / „Je ve vodě celý mokrý." Pros: gender is on the predicate proper. Cons: only usable where the pictured action makes the state clearly visible (few items), drops the action → weaker lex distractors, and adjective-state judgements raise ambiguity (FAIL risk). Use only as a supplementary minority on suitable items, never as the main minority.
- Rejected: neuter/plural (impossible with one-actor gendered pairs; -li/-ly not audible), conditional (meaning mismatch), passive (wrong role of pictured actor).

## Contrast kept by minority items
The grammatical distractor stays the mirrored opposite-gender actor doing the same action; the only cue distinguishing target and distractor is still the gender ending agreeing with the unexpressed subject (sám vs sama). The swap rule holds (v1 fem. l-participle ↔ v2 can target either gender; the mirrored picture becomes the target when v2 flips gender). Lex pictures remain wrong for both sentences because tense and „sám" do not change the action or object.
