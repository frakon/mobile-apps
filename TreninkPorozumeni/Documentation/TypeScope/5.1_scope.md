# 5.1 Reverzibilní věty – test-type scope

## Type
5.1 Reverzibilní věty (reversible sentences): two animate (or both movable) participants, either could do the action; the child must decode who does what to whom. Doc reference: Documentation/doc1, section 5.1. Retroactive rework under `_TestTypeScopeRule.md` (P18, 2026-10-02).

## Forms inventory
- Basic SVO, both cases visible: „Medvěd tlačí lva." – depictable: yes (used, majority).
- OVS, object fronted, accusative visible: „Medvěda tlačí lev." – depictable: yes (same picture as SVO; minority).
- Fronted prepositional object (OVS-like): „Na kluka stříká holčička." – depictable: yes (already present, counted as minority).
- SVO/OVS with instrument / place adjunct: „Maminka přikrývá babičku dekou.", „Maminku přikrývá babička dekou." – depictable: yes.
- Sentences where nominative = accusative (neuter, inanimate masculine, feminine consonant stems: „Kuře honí myš") – depictable: yes, but **ambiguous in OVS** → excluded (role decoding would rely on word order only).
- Passive reversible („Lev je tlačen medvědem.") – depictable: yes, but belongs to 5.4 → excluded here.
- Object relatives („Lev, kterého tlačí medvěd") – belongs to 5.7 → excluded.
- Pronoun objects („Medvěd ho tlačí") – need antecedent → not depictable unambiguously; 5.11 territory → excluded.

Acquisition: SVO with case is understood early (pre-school); object-first order with case-only cueing is mastered later and is a known source of errors in children with language disorders who rely on the "first noun = actor" strategy.

## Majority (marked) form
SVO with visible nominative/accusative endings („Bratr honí sestru.").

## Minority form(s)
OVS with visible accusative on the fronted noun („Sestru honí bratr."), including fronted prepositional object. Reason: it is fully natural Czech (topic-focus word order) and is exactly where the "first noun = doer" shortcut fails; a 100% SVO set would let children solve by word order alone. Starting hypothesis confirmed; correction: 27 of 100 items were already whole-OVS (both variants), so only 12 more mixed items were needed.

## Contrast kept by minority items
The grammatical distractor stays the role-reversed scene (same picture as for SVO). For an OVS sentence the child must use the case ending, not the position, to reject the reversed picture.
